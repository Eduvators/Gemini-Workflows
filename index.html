New chat starts a conversation. New notebook starts a source-organized workspace. Named notebook entries reopen your work. A conversation title reopens a chat. A menu label tells you where to go; a saved title tells you what you were working on.

The supplied list also contains a notebook titled “Copy of FDA Approves…”. This is a saved notebook entry, not a navigation feature. Its title alone does not verify its contents. Personal notebook and chat links are not included in this teaching guide.

Try it: a six-step menu exploration
  1. Open the sidebar and locate New chat, Library and Images. Say what you would use each one for.
  2. Open More and look for Customize and Skills. Return without changing a setting.
  3. Locate New notebook and All notebooks. Identify one saved notebook title. Do not open or share another person’s private materials.
  4. Inspect Scheduled if available. Identify where you would pause an unwanted schedule; you do not need to create one.
  5. Open Students if present. Record what your account actually offers. Do not assume another account has the same options.
  6. Explain why “Creating a New Skill” in a recent-chat list is different from the Skills management page.

01 · Get ready to teach with AI

Choose one small lesson you already teach. By the end, you will have a reviewed study resource, a reusable skill, and a sharing plan.

  1. Use a computer browser for this workshop. Open gemini.google.com and notebook.google.com in separate tabs. Sign in with your institution’s approved account and check the profile icon in both tabs.
  2. Gather two or three nonconfidential sources: your own lesson notes, an approved reading, and a learning objective. Remove student names, grades, identifying information, and private comments before using a file.
  3. Use Gemini for drafting, brainstorming and revising. Use Gemini Notebook to organize a lesson’s sources and develop resources from them. Sources supply information; prompts tell AI what you want done.
  4. Features depend on account, age, plan and administrator settings. If a feature is missing, use the fallback in each activity. Do not move institutional information into a personal account to bypass a restriction.
  5. Download the participant packet to record your choices. The website’s checkmarks are a convenience stored in this browser only; they are not a course submission.

Try this prompt

Help me plan a short lesson about [topic] for [learners]. The learning objective is [objective]. Suggest three ways AI could help me prepare. Identify the decisions I should make myself.

Evidence: You can explain which tool you will use first and name the learning outcome you want to support.

If you get stuck

If sign-in is blocked, work with the supplied fictional practice sources and draft prompts in the participant packet while your administrator checks access.

02 · Start a useful Gemini conversation

Treat your first response as a draft. Your teaching judgment shapes what happens next.

  1. Open a new Gemini chat. Enter the learner group, topic, objective, task, output format and boundaries. Replace every bracketed placeholder in the practice prompt.
  2. If you are attaching course material, use the add or attachment control and choose an allowed file or Drive item. Wait for its name to appear before sending. Tell Gemini which document to use.
  3. Read the response from a learner’s perspective. Look for unsupported facts, unclear vocabulary, invented standards and activities that require resources you do not have.
  4. Continue in the same chat to refine the draft: ask for a shorter explanation, a worked example, clearer directions or a second reading level. Start a new chat when you change to an unrelated lesson.
  5. Use Share & export below the response to export to Docs when available. Open the new document, edit it and set its sharing permissions. Copy into an approved document if export is unavailable.

Try this prompt

Act as a teaching partner. For beginning adult learners, draft a 15-minute activity on evaluating online information. Include one measurable objective, materials, numbered student directions, a worked example and a three-question exit ticket. Use plain language. Do not invent research findings or institutional policies. Label anything I need to verify.

Evidence: You have a revised activity that matches a measurable objective, plus one example of a change you made after reviewing the draft.

If you get stuck

If the answer is generic, add your course context and describe the intended student work. If a file is not recognized, check its contents and permissions or paste a short approved excerpt.

03 · Build your notebook inputs

Start with a small, intentional source collection. More files do not automatically make a better lesson.

  1. Open Gemini Notebook, choose Create new notebook, and name it for one unit or question, such as “Evaluating online information”. Choose Add sources.
  2. Choose an input offered in your account: upload a document, select a Drive file, paste text, or add a web or public captioned YouTube URL. Wait for processing, then open the source to inspect what arrived.
  3. Use readable names such as “A Lesson Notes” and “B Learning Objective”. For pasted text, include a descriptive title. Check scanned documents for readable text before relying on them.
  4. Web imports capture the page’s text, not every linked page or embedded video. YouTube imports use the transcript. Audio imports depend on transcription. Check missing visual context and specialized vocabulary yourself.
  5. Drive sources can auto-sync; open the source and use the sync control if needed. Recheck content after a change. For a local upload, keep a dated version and replace or reimport when you revise the original.
  6. For practice, select only Source A and ask a question. Then select A and B and ask it again. Write down how the evidence changes. Review discovered web sources before importing them.

Try this prompt

Using only the selected sources, identify the lesson’s central idea, three essential terms, and any missing information that would prevent a beginner from completing the task. Cite the source for each supported point. Do not fill gaps with guesses.

Evidence: Both sources open correctly. You can locate a specific sentence in each and explain what each contributes.

If you get stuck

If import fails, confirm permissions and file readability, try a smaller approved file, or paste the relevant text. A link to a login-only LMS page may not supply its content.

04 · Use the chat area with evidence

Move from an overview to a specific teaching question, then inspect the evidence behind the answer.

  1. In the Sources panel, check the materials you want to use. In Chat, type a focused question about those sources. For a source-only exercise, explicitly request no outside research or connected-app actions.
  2. Open a citation in the answer and read the passage around it. Ask: does the passage support the entire claim, or just part of it? Record a supported claim and one limitation.
  3. Ask a follow-up that changes one thing: audience, length, example, or format. Compare the revision with the original objective rather than accepting it because it sounds polished.
  4. Open Configure Chat to choose a style such as Learning Guide or Custom and adjust length. Save useful responses with Save to note so you can return to their citations.
  5. Newer experimental chat features can research, create files or use connected apps. Distinguish those actions from a source-grounded answer. Check what was used and supervise any action outside your source collection.

Try this prompt

Using only A Lesson Notes and B Learning Objective, explain the difference between a claim and evidence to a beginner. Give one example supported by the notes. Cite the relevant passage. Then ask me one question that checks understanding and wait for my answer.

Evidence: You have checked two claims against their citations and saved one useful response. You can say what the sources do not establish.

If you get stuck

If the answer misses a source, check its selection and access, name it in your prompt and narrow the question. If it cannot answer, investigate the gap instead of asking it to invent support.

05 · Create assets in Studio

An asset is an output you can use in teaching, such as a study guide, audio overview or slide deck. Choose it because it supports a learning goal.

  1. Confirm the notebook’s sources and your learning objective. Find Studio. Choose an output type. Where offered, open the pencil or customization control before generating.
  2. Begin with Reports, then Document, and choose a study guide or custom format. Specify audience, essential concepts, examples and a brief check for understanding. Interactive reports are a separate web option.
  3. For Audio Overview, choose the available format, language and length, then describe what the speakers should emphasize. Generate and listen to the full result before assigning it.
  4. For a Slide Deck, choose format, language, length and sources, then provide a focused brief. Open every slide to inspect labels and factual claims. Use the available revision controls or regenerate with clearer instructions.
  5. For Flashcards or Quizzes, customize topic and difficulty. Try the questions yourself and inspect explanations. Treat these as practice aids unless you have separately built and reviewed an assessment in your LMS.
  6. For an Infographic or Video Overview, specify a narrow teaching purpose. Check readable text, visuals and narration. Supply a text alternative for essential visual information and captions or a transcript for media.
  7. Open the completed item from Studio. Rename it clearly if that option is available. Use its menu for the export or download formats offered. Recheck the actual exported file, not only the on-screen preview.

Try this prompt

Create a beginner study guide from the selected sources about evaluating online information. Include: the learning objective, a plain-language explanation, five key terms, one worked example, three practice questions and a separate answer guide. Mark unsupported points as “not established by these sources”.

Evidence: You have one text resource and one different format, both reviewed for accuracy and accessibility.

If you get stuck

If Studio generation is unavailable or a quota is reached, ask Chat for a structured outline, copy it into an approved document and build the asset there. A generated file is not automatically an accessible file.

06 · Connect only what the task needs

Connectors are integrations. Google generally labels them Connected Apps. They can bring information from another service and, for some apps, take actions.

  1. In Gemini, open the available Connected Apps settings. Inspect the app description and supported actions. School accounts may require administrator approval and an eligible Workspace edition.
  2. For a first trial, choose an approved read-only task, such as locating your own lesson document in Drive. In the prompt, type @ and select the available app when offered; name the document precisely.
  3. Review connection permissions before proceeding. Verify the retrieved item’s title and contents. Do not assume that a matching filename means it is the correct version.
  4. In Gemini Notebook on the web, use Settings, then Connected apps to inspect integrations. In an individual notebook, Configure Chat lets you enable or disable an app for that notebook.
  5. A newly connected Notebook app is enabled across notebooks; per-notebook changes are separate. Mention the app in Chat when you want to use it. Check the notebook’s settings before a source-only exercise.
  6. For actions such as creating events or changing content, review destinations and details first. Use only institution-approved providers. Disconnect unused apps; do not add a custom connector during this beginner activity.

Try this prompt

Using the approved Drive connection, find my document titled [exact title]. Show the title and summarize its learning objective. Do not edit files, send messages, create events or change sharing permissions.

Evidence: You can name the service, the information it accessed, whether it could change anything, and how you would disable it.

If you get stuck

If your app is unavailable, upload or paste an approved copy of the needed material. This is a valid way to complete the lesson without a connector.

07 · Create a reusable teaching skill

A skill stores a repeatable way of working. Your prompt supplies today’s lesson; the skill supplies your recurring instructional expectations.

  1. In Gemini on the web, open Customize in the sidebar, or More first if needed. Choose Skills, then Create and Create manually. Availability depends on eligibility and account settings.
  2. Name the skill “Accessible lesson partner”. Add a description that explains when it should be used. Paste the sample instructions below, then create the skill.
  3. Test it on a familiar lesson. Explicitly select the skill using the shortcut shown in your account, such as / or @, or ask Gemini to use its name. Inspect whether the expected sections actually appear.
  4. Run a second test with a different subject. Check that the skill adapts to the topic while retaining plain language, logical headings and a human review step.
  5. Return to Skills to edit and save improved instructions. Deactivate the skill from its management menu when you do not want it applied automatically.
  6. If skill creation is unavailable, save these instructions in an approved document and paste them at the beginning of a new chat. To share the method with colleagues, share this instruction text and test it in each account; do not assume a built-in skill-sharing feature is present.

Try this prompt

Use this skill when I request a lesson or student activity. Ask for the learner group, topic and objective if missing. Produce: objective, needed materials, brief explanation, numbered activity steps, one worked example, practice and a check for understanding. Use clear headings and plain language. Describe meaningful visuals in text. Suggest captions or transcripts for media. Do not invent sources, standards or policies. Flag uncertain claims. Use fictional examples instead of student records. End with a short teacher review checklist. Keep answer keys separate from student directions.

Evidence: The skill produces useful structure on two different topics. You can identify one revision that improved its behavior.

If you get stuck

If the skill gives too much detail, specify the length. If it invents content, strengthen source instructions and review the result. Saved instructions guide behavior but do not guarantee it.

08 · Share the right output

Decide whether learners need a finished resource, the whole notebook, or a public demonstration of a chat. These are different sharing choices.

  1. For a finished Gemini response, use Share & export, then Export to Docs if offered. Edit the document, use its Share control and set the intended audience. A table may offer Export to Sheets.
  2. For a notebook report, use the item menu to export to Docs where available. Data Tables can export to Sheets. Exported files have their own permissions and later edits do not synchronize back to the notebook.
  3. For Studio media or slides, use the asset’s Share or Download control. Slides may offer PDF or PowerPoint. Download audio for an approved LMS when allowed. Add the accessible companion text before posting.
  4. To share a whole notebook privately, open Share, enter recipients and choose Viewer or Editor. Viewer is appropriate for reading; Editor allows changes. Review every included source before granting access.
  5. Chat View is a focused display, not a way to securely conceal the notebook’s underlying sources. Keep teacher-only answers, private notes and restricted materials out of a learner-shared notebook.
  6. A Gemini chat share link is public. Review the entire included conversation before creating one. Public notebook sharing is currently unavailable to Education and Enterprise accounts; use approved private sharing or reviewed exported files.
  7. Open your final link using the intended recipient’s account or an authorized test account. Check sign-in, viewing rights, downloads and accessibility. In Canvas LMS, use a descriptive link or upload the reviewed file. No iframe is needed.

Try this prompt

Review this teaching resource before I share it. Identify factual claims to verify, private information to remove, missing attribution, unclear student directions and accessibility barriers. Suggest concrete edits. Do not publish or change access settings.

Evidence: A recipient can open the intended material without gaining unintended access. Your packet records the audience, permission and tested link.

If you get stuck

If a link says access denied, check the recipient’s account and file permission. Do not make the resource public just to solve a school-account restriction.

09 · Finish a classroom-ready resource

Your final product should show both what AI contributed and what you checked as the teacher.

  1. Assemble a short lesson resource with a measurable objective, readable directions, a worked example and a check for understanding. Keep the answer guide in a separate teacher file if learners should not see it.
  2. Check important claims against the original course sources. Correct unsupported statements, misleading simplifications and mismatched citations. Verify any numbers, names or dates.
  3. Apply heading styles, descriptive links, meaningful image descriptions and adequate contrast. Check keyboard access for interactive materials. Provide equivalent text for audio and visual content.
  4. Add a brief disclosure suited to your institution: “Prepared with AI assistance. Content, examples and accessibility were reviewed and revised by the instructor.” Retain source attribution.
  5. Complete the participant packet’s final review and sharing record. Save the reviewed version with a useful name and date. Choose one class where you will try it and one sign of student learning to observe.

Try this prompt

Help me review my lesson against this objective: [objective]. What must students actually do to demonstrate learning? Identify any activity that does not support that objective. Suggest one improvement without adding new factual claims.

Evidence: You leave with a usable resource, evidence of review, a reusable workflow and a specific teaching next step.

If you get stuck

If the asset looks attractive but does not help learners demonstrate the objective, simplify it. A clear, accessible document is a successful outcome.

A Lesson Notes

Fictional workshop teaching notes, created for practice. A claim is a statement someone asks us to accept. Evidence is information offered to support that claim. A polished website is not evidence that a claim is true. When evaluating an online claim, identify the author, inspect the evidence, consider the date and context, and compare with another reliable source. An advertisement may provide useful facts, but its purpose is to sell. A personal story may describe one experience without establishing what usually happens. If the evidence is missing, record what you still need to know rather than guessing.

B Learning Objective

Fictional workshop objective and task. After this lesson, learners will evaluate an online claim by naming its author or organization, identifying the evidence offered, and proposing one independent check. Students will write three to five sentences. Success means distinguishing a claim from its evidence and explaining a limitation. Learners may respond in writing or give an equivalent recorded explanation with a transcript. No live student records are needed.

C Practice Scenario

Fictional scenario for evaluation, not a real product or research finding. A website selling a study app says: “Our app doubles learning speed.” Its evidence is a quote from one satisfied user. The page has no publication date, no research report and no description of how learning speed was measured. A learner should identify the seller as the source, recognize the testimonial as limited evidence, and ask for independent research and a clear measurement method before accepting the claim.
